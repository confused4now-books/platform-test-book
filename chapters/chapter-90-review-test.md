# Review test: how soil forms (platform test)

Soil is the thin, living skin of the land. It forms where rock, water, air and living things meet, and it changes slowly over thousands of years.

Weathering breaks rock into smaller pieces. Frost splits stone along its joints, and rain slowly dissolves minerals from its surface.

Plants and animals add organic matter. Roots open channels that let air and water in, and worms mix the upper layers as they feed.

Over time the soil develops bands called horizons. The topsoil is usually darkest, because it holds the most organic matter.

Climate shapes every one of these processes. Warm, wet places weather rock fast, while cold, dry places weather it slowly.

Farmers depend on healthy soil to grow food. Ploughing too often damages its structure and leaves it open to erosion, so many farmers now plough less (platform test).

Erosion by wind and water (changed meanwhile, platform test) can remove in a single season what took centuries to form.

Protecting soil means keeping it covered with plants, adding organic matter and disturbing it only when needed.
